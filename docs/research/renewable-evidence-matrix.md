# Renewable-effects evidence matrix — solar PV and wind turbines

> **Date:** 14 November 2026
> **Purpose:** Build a verifiable, primary-source evidence matrix for the environmental effects of solar photovoltaic (PV) and wind-turbine deployment, scoped to requirement R07 (`docs/REQUIREMENTS.md`): "Solar/wind environmental-effects research and supported simulations — Verified evidence matrix and scale-specific energy/wake/land-surface models; explicit unsupported effects."
> **What this document is:** Research context. It is not a project verdict, not a model selection, not an approval of any placement, and not an instruction to the application. It does not claim R07 is complete. It does not modify any requirement, source code, test, AGENTS.md or other document. It exists so that any future claim made in the product about PV/wind environmental effects can be traced to a primary source — or marked unsupported.
> **Placement of this material:** It supports later R07 work (scale-specific energy/wake/land-surface models and explicit unsupported effects). It does not authorize deploying any simulation, does not assert any effect for Bihar, and does not contradict or supersede `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md`, `docs/RESEARCH.md` or `docs/research/renewables-question.md`.

## How to read this document

- Every entry in the matrix cites numbered sources `[S-xx]`; the full bibliography with URLs and access status is in the Sources section. Sources retrieved 14 November 2026 unless noted.
- Evidence level is one of: **observed** (field/satellite measurement), **modeled** (numerical simulation, not field-validated for the claim), **theoretical** (analytic/idealized theory), **unsupported** (no traceable peer-reviewed or mission-agency evidence found).
- "Confidence" reflects replication and method breadth across independent teams, not plausibility.
- All quantitative findings are quoted with the units and (where reported) uncertainty/error bounds from the primary source. No number here should be taken as applying to Bihar without the applicability notes.
- Where this document could not access or verify a source or claim, it is listed in "What we could NOT verify" — absence of verification is not evidence of falsity.
- `docs/research/renewables-question.md` is a user-supplied research brief preserved as context. Its numerical claims were treated as hypotheses to check, not as facts. One of its headline claims (a 2025 modelling study reporting ~30–40% PV-array wind-speed reduction) has a plausible match in [S12]; the brief's other reported numbers (1.3 °C, ~2019, 1.5 m) match [S05]. Neither match validates the brief's framing; both still carry the limits noted below.

**Inclusion rules:**

1. Peer-reviewed journal articles, and government/mission-agency technical materials (e.g., US DOE AWAKEN fact sheet — see [S39]). Blogs, news articles, marketing, NGO commentary and conference proceedings were used only as pointers to primary work or to mark status (e.g., the non-peer-reviewed VayuMandal commentary on Li 2018, noted as such).
2. Numerical claims were verified against the primary text (abstract, full text, or publisher PDF) retrieved during this pass. Where the text was paywalled or blocked but the abstract was accessible, only abstract-level claims are used, and the source is tagged "abstract-verified".
3. If only secondary sources supported a claim, it is marked UNVERIFIED and says so.
4. No number has been invented. Where a paper was accessible, its statistics are quoted with units; where not, the claim is left out or marked.
5. Replication status was assessed from subsequent literature surfaced in search (e.g., conflicting field measurements for PVHI sign, satellite composites for wind farms).

## Scope and key framing

Six effect chains are covered, matching the brief's categories:

1. PV heat island / albedo / energy export
2. PV effects on near-surface temperature, humidity, boundary-layer wind, convection
3. Turbine momentum extraction and wakes; local-to-regional scaling laws
4. Hypothetical large-scale feedbacks (Sahara/Atlantic claims) and their contested status
5. Land-surface / ecosystem tradeoffs (land cover, roughness, runoff)
6. Floodplain/settlement-scale effects relevant to Bihar (25.7°N, Gandak/Sonpur alluvial floodplain)

**Baseline physics the evidence is consistent with:** turbines redistribute boundary-layer heat (they do not add heat); PV arrays change albedo and the surface energy balance (locally they can warm or cool, depending on surface, coverage, emissivity); exported electricity is not energy destroyed, it is heat released where it is used, not where it is generated; neither technology has an established pathway to cause or prevent river flooding.

## Explicitly unsupported or contested effects

The following cannot currently be claimed, in the app or in any document, on evidence gathered here:

1. **Flood increase or decrease caused by PV or wind installations** — no primary source supports any direction or magnitude at settlement/floodplain scale. (Chain 6.)
2. **PV arrays "trap" clouds / wind turbines suppress rainfall locally/regionally** — no peer-reviewed evidence found. Closest model results (Fitch 2015, slight low-cloud decrease downstream) are weak, sign-uncertain and model-only [S23].
3. **Solar farms universally cool their surroundings or universally warm them** — contradicted by mixed field signs (arid: warming air / cooling LST; UK grassland: summer soil cooling) [S04,S05,S06,S08,S09,S10]. Any single-sign blanket statement is wrong.
4. **Sahara result transferability** — Li 2018's >2× Sahel rainfall is a single fixed-SST model experiment, Sahara-specific per the authors, unvalidated by observation; coupled models (Lu 2021) keep local — add Amazon-drought remote harm; Long 2024 adds India PV-potential loss (~4 % annual / ±5–8 % JJA under S20 ~86 TW hypothetical) [S30,S31,S32]. None applies to Bihar or to any real deployment. "Sahara greening" is contested, model-only, hypothetical.
5. **Extrapolating within-array wind reductions (27–56 %) to landscape scale** — the cited values are station-scale sheltered-area findings [S11–S13]; regional extrapolation is unsupported; global blockage/IBL limits are the relevant large-scale constraint and yield small climate effects (±0.3 °C, 0–5 % precip, winter-only, Europe current+2020 policy) [S20].
6. **Global extraction limits** — "limitless wind" and "wind is nearly free because it renews" are both misstatements; credible limits cluster at ~1 We m⁻² (Adams & Keith 2013; Miller 2015/2016) [S25,S26]; saturation SWPP >250 TW (Jacobson & Archer) [S24]; surface ≥400 TW (Marvel) [S27] — these bound *planetary* totals, not local siting decisions.
7. **Wind farms materially change East Asia/Indian monsoon rainfall today** — modeled signals (±0.3–1 %) are smaller than interannual variability and model-dependent [S19,S20,S23]; no observational detection exists.
8. **Any numerical effect on Gandak/Sonpur specifically** — no instrumented study exists; every quantitative value above comes from elsewhere; locally they are hypotheses, not measurements.
9. **Barron-Gafford's 3–4 °C nocturnal PVHI as a universal figure** — contradicted/adjudicated by Broadbent 2019 (different site, no significant nocturnal difference) [S05]; scale-, biome- and sensor-height-dependent. The UK counter-case shows cooling sign in summer [S06].
10. **The brief's "2025 study, 30–40 % wind reduction" as a regional-effect finding** — the matching study is Sun et al. 2025 (J. Environ. Manag. 382:125395; CFD at one Zhangjiakou station; sheltered-area 27.6–42.3 %), not a regional observation [S12]. The brief's "1.3 °C, 2019, 1.5 m, undisturbed desert" matches Broadbent et al. 2019 (J. Appl. Meteor. Climatol. 58:989–1006) ✓ [S05].
11. **Claim that exported solar electricity "releases" its heat at the array** — false by conservation; Millstein & Menon 2011 and Hu 2016 make this explicit: heat appears at the *demand* site (mostly urban) [S07,S40].
12. **AWAKEN-derived wake/microclimate numbers for India** — the AWAKEN campaign is US (Great Plains), referenced in docs/RESEARCH.md but not independently verified in this pass [S39].

## What a supported simulation would require

These are the prerequisites for R07-supported adapters (not commitments, not capabilities claimed):

### Solar array energy/wake/land-surface model (local scale, ~10 m–2 km)
- Inputs: array geometry (rows, tilt, gap), coverage fraction; module efficiency and temperature coefficient; module albedo and emissivity; pre-deployment albedo/emissivity (land-cover class + measured if possible); ground-clearance height; land cover (ESA WorldCover); soil texture/moisture; local 1–2 yr albedo/LST baseline (MODIS/Landsat/Ecoss? — none currently connected per docs/DATA.md); boundary-layer profile (ERA5-class reanalysis or tower measurements).
- Model class: surface energy-balance scheme with explicit PV module layer (e.g., the Noah-MP/CLM-class PV parameterizations used in the WRF studies above), or site-validated CFD for row-scale flow (Sun 2025 approach), or LES for wake/roughness interaction. WRF-Solar is the cited model family for this class; tool selection pending expert review (docs/RESEARCH.md work-plan 6).
- Validation required before any quantitative claim: at least one season of eddy-covariance + soil-moisture + multi-height air/skin temperature at a proposed Bihar site (or nearest instrumented analog), vs control site; verification metric: albedo closure and net-radiation residual, not just air temperature. Broadbent/Armstrong-style QA (sensor aspiration, heights, PVHI detection) mandatory — Broadbent is the cautionary tale for PVHI sign/discrepancy.
- Resolution: row-scale (1–5 m) for panel shadow/flow or at least one-grid-cell-per-row; mesoscale (1 km) for farm-scale feedback; never below ~500 m for regional rainfall claims.
- What remains impossible without it: any Bihar-specific number; any claim about monsoon convection; any flood-relevant statement; any "X °C hotter/cooler" output in the UI.

### Wind turbine/scope model (local → mesoscale)
- Inputs: rotor diameter, hub height, power & thrust curves; layout (spacing/angles); inflow ABL profile & stability classification (radiosonde/ERA5/local tower); Coriolis (not negligible at Bihar latitude), surface roughness map (OSM/Copernicus land cover).
- Model tiers: (a) Jensen/Gaussian wake model for layout screening only; (b) LES/RANS for wake and IBL physics (Porté-Agel 2020 methods); (c) WRF with Fitch 2012/Fitch 2015 or Vautard 2014 parameterization for mesoscale; (d) coupled ESM (EC-Earth-class) only for hypothetical global/continental chains — not for local decisions.
- Validation: pre/post commissioning met-mast comparison (Bleeg 2018 protocol) or LiDAR scan of wake (AWAKEN-style, [S39] unverified-here); MODIS/ECOSTRESS LST composite before/after for observational detection ( Zhou 2012/Slawsky 2015/Qin 2022 protocol, with their caveats: fixed overpass, clear-sky bias).
- Required for a claim: error bars; at least one post-construction verification season; explicit decoupling of local (wake) vs farm-scale (blockage) effects (Bleeg/Sebastiani method).
- What remains impossible without it: any Bihar-specific energy or wake number; any regional rainfall statement; any wind farm EIA with magnitudes.

### Land-surface / hydrological interface (the Bihar flood question)
- Requires (a) and (b) above plus a hydrology/DEM coupling (e.g., the project's existing terrarium DEM + future hydrology work in docs/REQUIREMENTS.md R04/R05) — PV/wind effects enter flood models **only** via altered roughness, evaporation and infiltration parameters, each needing field measurement. None exists.
- What remains impossible, generally, without substantial new field data: causal attribution of any specific Bihar flood to PV/wind (sign unknown); anything about sediment, channel migration, or embankment interaction; ENSO-conditioned PV/wind flood effects.

### Absolute limits (regardless of effort)
- Global/continental chains (Chain 4) cannot be validated by any local measurement campaign crafted here; they are model-ensemble questions.
- Cloud microphysics ("trapping") cannot be resolved at current validated scales for this use.
- Teleconnection strength for India from Sahara-scale deployment remains irreducibly model-dependent; do not present it as prediction.
