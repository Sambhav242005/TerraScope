# Research Prompt: Can Large-Scale Solar Panels and Wind Turbines Change Earth's Wind, Weather, and Climate?

I want to explore a scientific hypothesis about renewable energy infrastructure and its unintended effects on the natural environment.

My central question is:

**If humans install enormous numbers of solar panels and wind turbines across Earth's surface, could extracting solar and wind energy alter natural energy flows enough to change local temperatures, wind speeds, atmospheric circulation, rainfall patterns, ecosystems, and potentially the global climate?**

I want a rigorous, physics-based investigation, not a generic explanation that renewable energy is good or bad.

## 1. My Original Reasoning

My reasoning started with two observations:

1. Wind turbines extract kinetic energy from naturally moving air to generate electricity. Therefore, the wind exiting a turbine should have less kinetic energy than the incoming wind, and the turbine should alter the surrounding airflow.
2. Solar panels convert incoming solar radiation into electricity. Without the panels, that solar radiation would have interacted with soil, vegetation, water, or other natural surfaces. Installing panels must therefore modify the local energy balance, potentially changing surface heating, convection, atmospheric pressure gradients, and wind formation.

I want to know whether these effects remain local or become important at regional and planetary scales as renewable energy infrastructure expands.

## 3. Can Solar Panels Change Wind?

Wind is driven by atmospheric pressure gradients, which are influenced by uneven heating, atmospheric circulation, Earth's rotation, terrain, and other physical processes.

My hypothesis is that large solar installations could modify local atmospheric heating and consequently influence wind formation and movement.

Investigate:

- Changes in surface-to-air heat transfer.
- Changes in convective boundary-layer development.
- Alterations to atmospheric stability and vertical mixing.
- Physical obstruction and surface roughness introduced by panel arrays.
- Changes in near-surface wind speed and turbulence.
- Potential development of local thermal circulations.
- Possible downstream effects beyond solar farm boundaries.
- Differences between small rooftop systems, utility-scale solar farms, and massive desert installations.

A previously discussed 2025 modelling study reportedly found wind-speed reductions of approximately 30–40% within a simulated photovoltaic-array environment. Verify the actual study, methods, measurement locations, applicability, and limitations before using this number.
