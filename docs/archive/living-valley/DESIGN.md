# Design — The Last Glasswing

Use a natural-history museum, fantasy valley and scientific laboratory atmosphere. Keep deep green, pale parchment surfaces and restrained warm accents. The habitat is the dominant visual element.

## Investigation journey

1. Cinematic introduction to the Glasswing and Moonveil Valley, followed by Begin investigation.
2. Explore the living valley, discover five evidence locations and record observations in the field journal.
3. Select among three explanations and attach at least two discovered clues.
4. Rewind before the collapse, change one ecological factor and run the alternative timeline.
5. Compare aligned original/alternative states and population traces; then assess evidence and rescue separately.

## Living 3D Valley

The approved enhancement replaces the flat habitat with a spatial valley containing woodland, meadow, stream and wetland. Show recognizable deer, rabbits, foxes, squirrels, songbirds, owls, fish, frogs, turtles and Glasswings. Population availability, vegetation and freshwater follow the selected simulation snapshot. Animal movement is decorative rather than ecological evidence or a fine-grained individual simulation.

Use an explorable camera and readable clue access without making precise camera gestures necessary to complete the investigation. Keep exactly one ecological moment visible per world. Comparison may show two labeled worlds at the same year. Do not restore the previous Quantum tree, multiple intervention combinations or object time tunnels.

Foxes and owls are ambient fauna. The intervention removes the separately modeled Glasswing predation pathway; it must not misleadingly erase every carnivore from the valley.

## Access and motion

Desktop places the world beside controls; mobile keeps the world prominent with reachable stacked panels. Real labeled buttons expose all five clues. Provide visible keyboard focus, touch-sized actions, text/data equivalents, and an accessible rendering fallback.

Respect reduced motion and allow ongoing decorative animation to pause. User-started simulation playback has separate pause/resume controls. Never show animals when their population is zero. Decorative density represents population and need not draw every organism.

Keep spoilers out of the introduction and uncollected clues. Final text distinguishes a correct causal hypothesis, evidence quality and intervention success.
